# AI Search Visibility Checklist

**Version:** 1.0  
**Maintainer:** [Touchstone Infotech](https://www.touchstoneinfotech.com/)  
**Professional review:** [Request an SEO Audit](https://www.touchstoneinfotech.com/services/seo-audits/)

This framework contains **100 checks**. Not every check applies to every website or organization.

---

## 1. Crawlability & Indexability
- [ ] Important public content is accessible to standard web crawlers.
- [ ] Priority pages return successful HTTP responses.
- [ ] Important pages are not accidentally blocked by robots.txt.
- [ ] Important pages do not contain unintended noindex directives.
- [ ] Canonical signals consistently identify preferred indexable URLs.

## 2. AI Crawler Accessibility
- [ ] robots.txt rules for relevant AI/search crawlers are intentionally configured.
- [ ] Crawler policies reflect the organization's actual content-use strategy.
- [ ] CDN, WAF and bot-protection systems are checked for unintended crawler blocking.
- [ ] Server/CDN logs are reviewed when crawler-access questions matter.
- [ ] Emerging files or conventions such as llms.txt are not relied on as substitutes for normal web accessibility.

## 3. Entity Identification
- [ ] The website clearly states the organization's official name.
- [ ] The About page clearly explains what the organization does.
- [ ] Core products, services and areas of expertise are unambiguous.
- [ ] Important people, brands, products and locations are described with sufficient context.
- [ ] Entity naming is consistent enough to reduce ambiguity with similarly named organizations.

## 4. Brand Consistency
- [ ] Core brand name and description are consistent across major owned properties.
- [ ] Contact and location information is accurate across authoritative profiles.
- [ ] Major social and professional profiles use recognizable brand identity.
- [ ] Outdated business descriptions are corrected where the organization controls them.
- [ ] Acquisitions, rebrands or name changes are explained clearly where relevant.

## 5. Organization & Author Signals
- [ ] Important editorial content identifies an appropriate author or responsible organization.
- [ ] Author pages provide relevant expertise and background where useful.
- [ ] Organization information is easy to verify from the website.
- [ ] Editorial ownership and review responsibility are clear for high-stakes content.
- [ ] Organization/Person structured data is accurate where implemented.

## 6. Topic Authority
- [ ] The site covers priority subjects beyond a handful of isolated articles.
- [ ] Supporting content answers important subtopics around core expertise.
- [ ] Content architecture connects related concepts logically.
- [ ] Major topic gaps compared with credible competitors are identified.
- [ ] Old or weak content is updated, consolidated or removed when appropriate.

## 7. Answer-First Content
- [ ] Pages answer the primary question clearly and early.
- [ ] Important definitions are concise and understandable without excessive context.
- [ ] Headings accurately describe the information beneath them.
- [ ] Complex topics use lists, steps, tables or examples when these improve comprehension.
- [ ] Content avoids unnecessary filler before delivering useful information.

## 8. Information Gain
- [ ] Priority content adds something beyond generic summaries already common online.
- [ ] Original observations, examples or frameworks are included where appropriate.
- [ ] Content reflects actual subject-matter knowledge rather than superficial rewriting.
- [ ] Comparative content explains meaningful differences rather than repeating specifications.
- [ ] Important pages provide sufficient depth to resolve the user's likely follow-up questions.

## 9. First-Party Expertise
- [ ] Real experience is demonstrated through examples, workflows or case evidence where possible.
- [ ] Original research or internal data clearly explains its source and methodology.
- [ ] Case studies distinguish measured outcomes from assumptions or projections.
- [ ] Expert commentary is attributed to identifiable people or teams.
- [ ] Proprietary processes are explained enough to provide genuine informational value.

## 10. Evidence & Citation Readiness
- [ ] Important factual claims can be independently verified.
- [ ] Statistics identify credible sources or methodology.
- [ ] Claims that can become outdated include dates or appropriate context.
- [ ] Primary sources are preferred when supporting important factual statements.
- [ ] Quotations, research and third-party data are attributed clearly.

## 11. Structured Data
- [ ] Structured data accurately represents visible page content.
- [ ] Organization markup uses correct identity information.
- [ ] Article, Product, LocalBusiness or other relevant types are used only when appropriate.
- [ ] SameAs links point to legitimate identity profiles when used.
- [ ] Structured data is validated and monitored for errors after deployment.

## 12. Content Architecture
- [ ] Priority entities and topics have clear canonical destination pages.
- [ ] Related articles link to relevant cornerstone or service content naturally.
- [ ] Orphaned high-value pages are identified and connected.
- [ ] Breadcrumbs or hierarchy cues clarify relationships where useful.
- [ ] Duplicate pages competing to explain the same concept are consolidated when appropriate.

## 13. Third-Party Authority
- [ ] The brand is represented on credible third-party sites relevant to its market.
- [ ] Important professional profiles are complete and accurate.
- [ ] Industry associations, certifications or partnerships are verifiable where claimed.
- [ ] High-quality independent mentions are tracked over time.
- [ ] Authority building prioritizes relevance and credibility rather than link volume alone.

## 14. Digital PR & Mentions
- [ ] The organization has genuinely newsworthy or expert-led stories to pitch.
- [ ] Original research/data is packaged so journalists and publishers can understand it.
- [ ] Expert commentary is available for relevant media opportunities.
- [ ] Brand mentions are monitored for inaccurate or outdated information.
- [ ] Paid placements are not misrepresented as independent editorial endorsement.

## 15. Community & UGC Presence
- [ ] Relevant community discussions are monitored for recurring audience questions.
- [ ] The organization participates transparently where genuine expertise can help.
- [ ] Community participation avoids fabricated reviews, fake personas and spam.
- [ ] Real customer questions are used to improve owned-site content.
- [ ] Repeated misconceptions about the brand or category are addressed with useful public information.

## 16. Multimedia Visibility
- [ ] Important video/audio content has descriptive titles and supporting text.
- [ ] Video pages explain the subject beyond embedding the media alone.
- [ ] Transcripts or useful summaries are provided when appropriate.
- [ ] Visual assets use meaningful context, captions or alt text when relevant.
- [ ] Major educational content is repurposed across formats only when each format adds user value.

## 17. Google AI Search Readiness
- [ ] Core pages follow normal Google Search crawl and indexability fundamentals.
- [ ] Priority informational content directly resolves complex user questions.
- [ ] Important claims are supported with trustworthy evidence.
- [ ] Pages demonstrate clear topical context and entity relationships.
- [ ] Google Search Console is monitored for query/page trends around priority topics.

## 18. LLM Answer Visibility
- [ ] A defined test set covers informational, commercial and brand-related questions.
- [ ] ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity are tested separately rather than treated as one system.
- [ ] Tests record whether the brand is mentioned, linked or cited.
- [ ] Competitors appearing repeatedly are documented for comparative analysis.
- [ ] Visibility findings are investigated at the source/content/authority level before changes are made.

## 19. Prompt Monitoring
- [ ] Priority prompts are based on real audience questions rather than vanity queries alone.
- [ ] Prompt wording is kept consistent for longitudinal comparisons.
- [ ] Location, personalization and other relevant test conditions are documented.
- [ ] Multiple observations are used before declaring a meaningful visibility change.
- [ ] Prompt sets are reviewed periodically as products, markets and user behavior change.

## 20. Measurement & Governance
- [ ] AI visibility is tracked separately from conventional organic rankings.
- [ ] Referral traffic from AI platforms is monitored where analytics data permits.
- [ ] Brand mentions/citations are recorded with dates and source URLs when available.
- [ ] Content changes made for AI visibility are documented for later evaluation.
- [ ] The team avoids promising guaranteed AI citations or rankings to stakeholders.

## Suggested priority model

**Critical:** discovery/access problems or serious entity misinformation.  
**High:** major authority, evidence or content gaps affecting priority topics.  
**Medium:** meaningful optimization opportunities.  
**Low:** incremental improvements and cleanup.
